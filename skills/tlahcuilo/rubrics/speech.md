# Rubric — Spoken script / presentation narration

For copy that will be **read aloud** to a live audience: presentation narration, demo voiceover,
remarks to a head of institution. The reader's test is not "does this read well" but "does this
*say* well" — a speaker must get through it without stumbling, and a listener must follow it once,
in real time, with no ability to re-read. Every panelist scores against these criteria and argues
from them; a position that doesn't map to a criterion is out of scope.

## Criteria

1. **speakable** — Can a presenter deliver each sentence in one breath without tripping? Flag
   consonant pile-ups, tongue-twisters, long subordinate chains, parentheticals that lose the
   thread, and any sentence whose grammar only resolves at the end. Punctuation must map to actual
   pauses a speaker takes.
2. **hearable-once** — Can a listener follow it in real time with no rewind? Flag ambiguous
   antecedents ("it", "this", "that"), numbers or names that arrive too fast, and any sentence that
   requires holding two clauses in memory before the meaning lands.
3. **no-echo** — Spoken repetition is far more conspicuous than written repetition. Flag any phrase,
   construction, or transition that recurs (the same opener twice, the same time-marker three
   times), unless the echo is a deliberate, earned callback.
4. **rhythm-varied** — Sentence lengths vary; the cadence does not lull. Flag runs of identical
   length or structure, and flag the opposite failure: choppy fragments with no long sentence to
   carry momentum.
5. **lands-plainly** — Each paragraph and the close land on a concrete, defensible point. Flag
   applause-line cadence, sloganeering, abstract triads, and vague uplift that sounds impressive
   aloud but says nothing checkable.
6. **credible-aloud** — Claims survive a skeptical listener in the room. Flag absolutes, unearned
   superlatives, and anything the speaker could be challenged on in the Q&A that follows.
7. **oral-transitions** — The listener always knows where they are: what is on screen, what just
   happened, what comes next. Flag missing bridges between the demo and the argument, and stage
   assumptions the audience can't see.

## Scoring (joust mode)

Score each criterion 1–5: **5** exemplary · **4** solid, minor gaps · **3** usable, real gaps ·
**2** the speaker will stumble or the listener will lose it · **1** absent/broken.

## What debate should surface here

- A sentence that cannot be said in one breath.
- A pronoun whose referent is clear on the page but ambiguous in the ear.
- The same phrase or time-marker recurring within a few sentences.
- Grandiose or abstract cadence standing in for a concrete claim.
- A missing bridge into or out of a live demo.
- A claim that invites a question the speaker cannot answer.

## Voice note

Spoken register: plain, concrete, short declaratives, no oratory. See `voices/speech.md`.
